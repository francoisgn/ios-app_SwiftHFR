Sources and renders are on draft PR #1382 (`docs/assets/source/concepts/`, commit `9de8d61`). Each concept is shown as the hero thumbnail, then its social-preview card. ### A. The lens (spirit of the current hero)

![A hero](https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/a-lens-hero.png?raw=true)

<img src="https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/a-lens-social.png?raw=true" width="480" alt="A social card">

**Idea.** The forum page and the phone stay, the genie effect goes: the web page is a printed sheet on an HFR-orange table, and the phone lies on it like a reading glass, showing the same categories natively. **Why less conventional.** No morphing, no dark gradient with a glow: one flat colour, one perspective, and the story (same forum, native) is told by the two objects touching. **Risks.** Still « a web page and an iPhone », so the least surprising of the three; the page's small text turns to texture at card size (only the HARDWARE.FR banner and the phone stay readable); the heaviest file (the JPEG will need care to stay under 500 KB); keeps the device frame and the third-party banner.

### B. `:o` (typographic poster)

![B hero](https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/b-smiley-hero.png?raw=true)

<img src="https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/b-smiley-social.png?raw=true" width="480" alt="B social card">

**Idea.** The forum's own smiley code, the one behind the app icon, typed in monospace and blown up as a poster in two inks (HFR orange, black on paper), with the wordmark set like a magazine cover and one footer line that tells the whole project: `[quote]le forum[/quote] → struct Forum: View`. **Why less conventional.** No device, no screenshot, no gradient: it speaks to the community first (anyone from HFR reads `:o` at once) and to developers second. Strongest at small sizes: the card is readable at 200 px wide, and the file is tiny (under 100 KB). **Risks.** An outsider sees an abstract shape before a forum app, and nothing shows the UI (the README's screenshots would carry that); the paper background is a light block on a dark README (works, but it is the least « dark-native »); « A forum from 1997 » rests on the copyright line of the captured home page (`hfr.webp`, « 1997-2025 »): to confirm or reword before the final.

### C. One list, six skins (themes)

![C hero](https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/c-skins-hero.png?raw=true)

<img src="https://github.com/ezzz/HFRnow/blob/9de8d61e8c07877f59b70c732c7a0e26383b5001/docs/assets/source/concepts/c-skins-social.png?raw=true" width="480" alt="C social card">

**Idea.** A single topic list runs across the whole image, and six slanted bands re-skin the part they cover with the app's own themes, colours taken from `docs/assets/themes/tokens/themes.json`: Swift, Legacy clair, Legacy sombre, Spooktober, Noël abusé, Rétro 5250. The titles are invented and written in the forum's voice. **Why less conventional.** The image is the UI itself, edge to edge, with no device frame and no third-party logo; it shows what only this app has (the themes, the terminal skin) and the forum's tone. **Risks.** The busiest of the three: at card size the wordmark and the bands read, the row text does not; it promises themes that are not all shipped as drawn (seasonal ones live only inside their window, Swift and Rétro ship as colours only), so the final would name only shipped themes or the operator accepts the preview; French row titles on an English README.

### My recommendation
**B** for the social card and the hero if the goal is « not conventional »: it is the only one that could not belong to another app. **C** if the hero must show the product. **A** is the safe evolution. A mix is possible: B as the social preview, C as the README hero (two sources to maintain).
