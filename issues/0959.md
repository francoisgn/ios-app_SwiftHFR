### Summary
As a reader who lands on a message and then taps the status bar to go to the top, I want to stay at the top, so that the reader does not pull me back to the anchor.

### What the code does
`WebKitResetWatch` landed in #915. When the scroll view is already at its top when the document is ready, the watch runs the landing at once and keeps it as a "second chance" (`App/SwiftHFR/Reader/WebKitResetWatch.swift`, `perform` / `moved`):
- The next time the scroll view arrives at its top, the watch takes that for WebKit's reset and runs the landing again.
- The second chance ends only at the page's first position report (`pageReportedPosition`) or when the user scrolls (`isTracking || isDragging || isDecelerating`).

Two ways it can go wrong:
- **Status-bar tap.** It scrolls the view to its top with an animation, which is not tracking, dragging or decelerating. Unverified: it may be taken for the reset.
- **Long window.** A landing pinned by images that are still loading sends no position report (`reader.js`, `landOnPost` / `releasePin`), so the window can last as long as the images load.

This is what failed `ReaderLandingWebTests.aDocumentLandsOnItsAnchorAndReportsTheTopmostMessageOnceScrollingStops` in #934: the test went back to the top before the report, and only message 2 was ever reported. #958 fixes the test, not this product path.

**Setup**
- `dev-swift` c78a1a56, with temporary trace prints in `WebKitResetWatch` (not committed).
- A temporary hosted test drives a real `ReaderWebController` / `WKWebView` on the iOS 27.0 simulator (iPhone 18 Pro), under 10 busy loops on the 10-core Mac.
- Document: two 3,000 px messages, anchor on message 2.
- The test waits until the scroll view shows the landing (offset > 2,000), then does what a status-bar tap does: an animated `setContentOffset` to the top, with no touch. It samples the offset for 3 s.

| Case | Runs | Back at the top and stays | Landed again |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tap right after the landing | 20 | 20 | 0 |
| Same, an image above the anchor answering in 6 s (the pin holds the position report back) | 20 | 20 | 0 |

**Why** (from the traces of the 40 runs):
- In 37 runs the landing took the "second chance" path: the scroll view was at its top at ready, so the landing ran at once and the watch stayed open.
- In all 37, WebKit's real reset arrived **a few milliseconds later** and used up that second chance. The landing ran once more at the reset, then the watch ended, before the scroll view had even shown the message.
- In the other 3 the reset came before ready, and no second chance was opened.
- So at the moment a reader can tap, the watch is always closed (`token == nil` at the tap, 40/40). The image pin delays the position report, but not the reset, so the "long window" does not open either.

**What is left in theory:** a reset that never arrives after a ready at the top would keep the second chance open until the first position report. That order (the reset applied before the navigation's commit reaches `start`) never occurred in these 40 runs, nor in the 100 runs for #995. No code change: a guard against an order not observed would be untested.
