### Method

1. **Build the reference, chronologically.** Gather the UI/UX recommendations of:
   - the Human Interface Guidelines (current pages);
   - the WWDC24, WWDC25 and WWDC26 design and UI sessions (UIKit, SwiftUI, Liquid Glass and new design system, navigation, bars, tab bars, sheets, menus, typography, accessibility, iPad and iPhone Duo layouts);
   - the UIKit and SwiftUI documentation for the behaviours they describe.

   **When several sources cover the same subject, keep only the most recent recommendation**, and note what it supersedes. Every rule cites its source: HIG page URL, or WWDC session URL with its timestamp. Nothing comes from memory (AGENTS.md §3 rule 1 applies to Apple too).
2. **Audit the app** (`App/SwiftHFR`) against that reference, screen by screen:
   - lists and rows;
   - navigation and top bars (see #865: the title scrolls away);
   - tab bar;
   - reader chrome;
   - composer;
   - sheets, alerts and toasts (see #864: auto-dismiss);
   - settings;
   - empty, loading and error states;
   - iPad split view and iPhone Duo;
   - Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, contrast, reduced motion.

   Read the code and, where needed, ask for a Mac capture: do not guess the rendering.
3. **Check every finding against legacy parity first.** M4 is legacy parity (`87fe0d1c`). A gap that legacy shares is still filed, but marked "(b) evolution, M5" unless the HIG makes it a defect (accessibility, broken platform behaviour).
