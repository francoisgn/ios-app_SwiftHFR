## Problem
In a new MP, the formatting bar moves up and down while the member goes recipient → subject → text. This is the operator's report, #592 point 2.

## Cause
Read from the code; the Mac check below confirms it on a device.
- `ReplyComposerView` laid the bar out as a plain `VStack` child next to the fields, so it moved with every re-layout of the stack.
- The bar stayed enabled while the recipient or subject field had focus, and every bar action ran `focused = .text`.
  - A tap from the recipient field inserted into the hidden text and moved focus to it.
  - The keyboard then changed height, and the bar moved with it. The recipient field has `autocorrectionDisabled`, so it likely shows no predictive bar and its keyboard is shorter.

## Change
- New `Composer/ComposerBarFocus.swift` holds pure rules over a new `ComposerField`, which was the view's private `Field`:
  - `allowsBar(focused:)` is off for `.recipient` and `.subject`, and on for `.text` and for no focus.
  - `focus(afterBarActionFrom:)` returns `.text` from `.text` or nil, and leaves any other field unchanged, so a bar action never takes focus from another field.
- `ReplyComposerView`:
  - The bar moves out of the stack into `.safeAreaInset(edge: .bottom, spacing: 0)` on the content.
  - The rule disables the bar, and the bar dims to 0.4 while disabled, because `.plain` buttons draw no disabled state (Fable, round 1).
  - Its refocus goes through the rule.
  - The bar itself is not restyled: `FormattingToolbar.swift` is untouched (owned by #598).
- **Why `safeAreaInset`, not `ToolbarItemGroup(placement: .keyboard)`**: the keyboard placement is a UIKit input accessory.
  - It is rebuilt for each focused field (recipient, subject, text), which is itself a jump at each focus change.
  - It would show the bar above all three fields.
  - It disappears without an on-screen keyboard: an iPad hardware keyboard, or a reply whose keyboard went away. That would change the reply composer.
  - The system lays out its content (horizontal scroll, palette).

  The inset is one view kept across focus changes. It sits on the keyboard safe area and no longer shares the stack's layout with the fields and banners.
- **Deviation from the brief, on purpose**: "enable only while the text has focus" is read as "off while another field has focus".
  - With no focus, the bar stays on, as today. Otherwise a reply whose focus went away (a sheet closed without inserting) would get a dead bar, and the reply composer must stay unchanged.
  - A tap then focuses the text, which takes focus from no field.
  - Fable agrees. The operator confirms; switching to the strict reading is one line plus two assertions.
- No GAP row: legacy had no BBCode bar, only the edit menu, and GAP-085 already records the bar as a UX change. The pointer comment is updated.

## Tests (`ComposerBarFocusTests`, Swift Testing)
- `theBarIsOffWhileAnotherFieldHasFocus` (recipient, subject)
- `theBarIsOnWhileTheTextHasFocusOrNothingHas`
- `aBarActionNeverMovesFocusOffAnotherField` (recipient, subject)
- `aBarActionKeepsOrBringsFocusToTheText`

Without the change, these symbols do not exist. I ran mutations of the rule locally (Linux `swiftc`, same assertions):
- "bar always on" (the behaviour before #599) fails the off-field checks;
- "a tap always focuses the text" (the refocus before #599) fails the focus checks.

The app target does not build on Linux, so CI is the arbiter. `swiftlint lint --strict` is clean on `App/SwiftHFR/Composer/` and on the test file.

## Open point (not changed, needs the device)
The recipient field has `autocorrectionDisabled`, so it likely shows no QuickType row and its keyboard is shorter. A bar pinned above the keyboard follows that height difference at the recipient → subject step. Changing it means changing the fields' keyboard traits, which is the operator's call. This PR removes the bounce caused by a bar tap, not that step.
