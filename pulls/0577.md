## Problem
The only boundary between reader messages was `border-bottom: 1px solid var(--separator)` (≈1.5:1 light, ≈1.8:1 dark) and a 3 % header tint. Legacy drew a near-black 1 px rule (`style-liste-light.css:153-160` @ 2440d79); the port weakened it without a GAP row.

## Change (`reader.css` only, plus test and docs)
- Tokens derived from the theme, declared on `:root`:
  - `--alt-strength`: 5 % light, 9 % dark, 7 % / 16 % with Increase Contrast.
  - `--rule-strength`: 25 % light, 35 % dark, 50 % with Increase Contrast.
  - `--surface-alt = color-mix(in srgb, var(--text) var(--alt-strength), var(--background))`.
  - `--separator-strong = color-mix(in srgb, var(--text) var(--rule-strength), var(--background))`. It is deliberately **not** built on `--app-dark-separator`: the app sets that variable on every reader, so the rule would equal the quiet separator in dark.

  Everything follows light, dark, the brightness slider and the classic style (classic changes no token).
- `article.message:nth-of-type(even)` takes `--surface-alt` (the dashed `div.separator1` does not shift the alternation). `.message` padding is `1em 1em .85em` and its bottom rule uses `--separator-strong`.
- On an even message, small text must not sit on a tint over a tint, so everything that has its own surface is composed over `--background`:
  - plain quote/code tables: `background: var(--background)`;
  - `quote-me`, `quote-allowed`, `quote-blocked`: the same tint as a gradient layer over `--background`;
  - classic header band: the 15 % accent as a gradient layer over `--background`.

  All use `:where()` or equal specificity and come after the rules they override, so the visibility tints and the classic whitelist/`.me` rules still win. Modern headers and `.content` tints are translucent and compose over the surface as before.
- Deviation from the issue: light Increase Contrast uses **7 %**, not 10 %, because `--secondary` on a 10 % surface is 4.27:1 (< 4.5).

Rule against the background: light 1.71:1 (was 1.5), dark 2.82:1 (was 1.8).

The classic header band over `--background` gives the same date contrast as on an odd message (the pre-PR value). The fallback accent `#007aff` alone gives 4.30:1 on any message, odd or even; the real value depends on the user's accent colour, hence the Mac check below.

## Tests
`ReaderSeparationWebTests` (real `WKWebView` via `ReaderWebTesting`, window forced light/dark, modern and classic style). The probe reads `getComputedStyle` of article 1 and 2 and returns rounded channels (`color-mix()` can compute to `color(srgb …)`). RGB may differ by 1; alpha, the border width and the tint-layer flags are exact.
- Light and dark, modern: backgrounds, bottom rule, quiet `--separator` (as a border colour, since the app supplies rgb()), plain quote, folded `quote-blocked` colour and tint layer, no header tint layer.
- Light and dark, classic: the same, plus the even header's colour (`--background`) and its tint layer.
