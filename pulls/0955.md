## What and why
- **Problem**: a Plus or Pro Max iPhone in landscape is regular width, so each tab's `NavigationSplitView` (Catégories, Favoris, Messages) showed the list and the reader side by side.
- **Legacy** (`87fe0d1c`): `MainWindow.swift:1380-1388` (`usesSidebarRoot`: split only when regular in both dimensions, or iPad) and `:1792-1847` (`rootContainer`, `rootTabs`): a landscape Pro Max (regular width, compact height) keeps the tab root, one `NavigationStack` per tab (`Favorites.swift:665`), the topic pushed full width with the tab bar hidden (`MessagesView.swift:4024`).
- **Change**: `TabSplitWidth` (in `RootNavigationView.swift`), applied by each tab's own split (`CategoriesTabView`, `FavoritesTabView`, `MessagesRootView`), gives it a compact width on an iPhone of compact height (`RootNavigationView.tabSplitSizeClass`). The split collapses to legacy's stack; the reader then hides the tab bar by its existing rule (`ReaderTabBar`, GAP-008). `MessagesRootView` now takes its reader and composer instead of `AppEnvironment`, so a test can build it.
- **Why no wider**: an iPad (Slide Over, Split View) and an iPhone regular in both dimensions (a foldable's inner screen, GAP-215) keep their width. The iPad sidebar root is untouched. The Plus readers keep the regular width and the tab bar on a landscape Pro Max, the operator's decided difference of GAP-008 (UXD-02, #424).
- Comments and docs follow (`TopicReaderView`, `ReaderPageControls`, `ComposerPlacement`, LOT-082 UXD-24 and GAP-214, LOT-083 GAP-008).
