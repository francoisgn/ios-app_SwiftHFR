## What and why
**Problem.** Now and then, reader web tests wait about 240 s for a position report after a programmatic scroll back to the top, then fail. `ReaderLandingWebTests.aDocumentLandsOnItsAnchorAndReportsTheTopmostMessageOnceScrollingStops` failed this way twice, each time with `reported: [2]` (job 133110414600 on #924, job 133160363316 on #936).

**Likely cause (landing test): a race with `WebKitResetWatch`'s second chance.** The watch was added by #915 (3f0ce59, 2026-11-16 02:10).
- The test starts with the scroll view at its top. At ready, the watch runs `landOnPost(2)` at once and keeps it as a second chance.
- The second chance ends only at the page's first position report, which comes 500 ms after scrolling stops.
- The test went back to the top as soon as `t2` was at 0, inside those 500 ms. The scroll view arriving at its top was taken for WebKit's late reset: the landing ran again, and only 2 was ever reported.

This mechanism is read from the code, not observed. A failure message now says whether the watch still runs, which would confirm it.

**Evidence**
- Both failing heads (68d380ef, 9fb3e7ab) contain #915. The passing run 51da4c09 contains it too, as a timing race would.
- **Not a WebContent restart.** In both runs the test's own WebContent process (pid 22809 in …316, pid 40651 in …600) never terminates: it is only frozen after the test ends.
- **The page was alive.** Both `evaluate` calls before the wait answered: the landing check `top == 0`, then the `scrollTo(0, 0)` itself.
- **`reader.js` is not at fault in this test.** The document has no images, so `settled()` is true, nothing is pinned, and a scroll that stays put is reported after the debounce.

**A second, unexplained loss.** The page-end test caught a different one on job 133090341114: the way back to the top was sent once and never applied, and the scroll view stayed at its end. That test has no anchor, so the watch plays no part, and why the scroll was lost is not established.

## Change
Test code only:
1. **Landing test.** It waits for the report of 2 before going back. This is stricter, and it is what the test's name says.
2. **`scrollPage(toScrollY:in:tolerance:timeout:)`** in `ReaderWebTestSupport.swift`. It resends `window.scrollTo` until the page's `scrollY` **and** the scroll view's `readingTop` are both at the target. It is used for:
   - the landing test's way back;
   - `ReaderInsetsWebTests.scrollPage` (its first scroll keeps its own answer time, then the same 15 s deadline);
   - the page-end test's 20-point way back.
3. **`scrollState(of:)`** is added to the failure messages: the page's `scrollY`, the scroll view's reading top, and the watched token.

**Not loosened.**
- Every report assertion is unchanged.
- No timeout is longer.
- Tolerances are unchanged: 1 pt, and 2 pt for the page-end test as before.
- Nothing is skipped or quarantined.

**Product side.** A status-bar tap to the top right after a landing may hit the same second chance and land again. That is #959 (`host: mac`).
