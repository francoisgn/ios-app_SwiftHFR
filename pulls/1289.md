### Problem
A PR body could pass the Issue and claim check and still be invisible to the sweep and the ledger (`LOT-012 · Closes #45`, `- Closes #77`, `**Closes #12**`, `Closed #9`; `Fixes #123` for the ledger). With `Refs #893 ...` before `Closes #50`, the ledger took 893 as the PR's issue and the check required #893 to be claimed.

### Cause
Three regexes: `issue_claims.ISSUE_LINK` (anywhere in the body, `Refs` included), `hygiene.CLOSES`/`CITES` (line start only, no decoration), `ledger.ISSUE` (no `fixes`, first match wins even a `Refs`).

### Change
`tooling/common/pr_refs.py` (stdlib only), imported by the three scripts:
- a reference opens a line after optional decoration (list marker, quote, bold/code marks, `LOT-XXX ·`), or a clause after `. ; , |`; keywords close/fix/resolve (all forms) and `Refs`; `Closes #50, #51` gives both; HTML comments and CR ignored; a keyword inside a sentence (`also fixes #12 in passing`) is prose.
- `closing_issues`, `refs_issues`, `cited_issues`, `linked_issues` (closing, else `Refs`), `primary_issue` (first closing ref, never a `Refs` when a `Closes` exists).
- `issue_claims.linked_issues` = `pr_refs.linked_issues`; hygiene `close` = `closing_issues`, hygiene `claim` = `cited_issues` (unchanged meaning); `ledger.issue_number` = `primary_issue`, else the PR number.
- Why one grammar, strict: the check accepts exactly what the others read. Two existing check tests changed on purpose: `refs #2` next to closing refs no longer counts (`test_linked_issues_dedupes_and_keeps_order`; `test_unclaimed_closed_missing_and_pull_request` uses `closes #4`).
- Not touched: master, workflow triggers.
