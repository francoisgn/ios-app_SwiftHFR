The change is limited to operator-run capture tooling. Normal cleanup protects existing session cookies and removes temporary cookies before followed redirects. However, temporary cookies can briefly reach persistent storage, allowing abrupt termination or overlapping runs to defeat their intended POST-only lifetime.

**Retained concerns**
- **Low · security · inferred:** The new form-cookie path inherits save-before-cleanup ordering: a nonredirecting POST can persist temporary cookies before finally removes them. Abrupt termination after that save, or an overlapping invocation loading the jar, can carry those cookies into later authenticated requests. This helper-level weakness predates the PR in GET captures, but the new caller extends it to the advertised POST-only lifecycle.

**Security Blast Radius**
- _inferred_ — The demonstrated exposure is bounded to operator-selected account state and destinations matching the cookie scope. Cookie directories are restricted to the owner, and jar writes use owner-only permissions. Broader tenant, service, infrastructure, or credential authority is not demonstrated by this change.

**Security Findings and Attack Paths**
- _inferred_ — The supported failure path is temporary-cookie insertion, a nonredirecting POST, request-level jar serialization, interruption before final cleanup, and reload by a later invocation. This establishes a lifecycle concern, not a verified credential-theft or privilege-escalation attack.

**Trust Boundaries and Controls**
- _observed_ — Before insertion, the form flow rejects names already held by the poster's jar, protecting stored session cookies from replacement and subsequent deletion. The loopback test asserts POST presence, absence on form and redirected GETs, later-request absence, and rejection of an md_user collision without another POST.

**Resilience and Maintainability Implications**
- _inferred_ — Cleanup is repeatable because removal tolerates an absent cookie, and finally covers ordinary request failures and exception unwinding. It does not make persistence atomic: jar writes truncate the file directly, with no coordination between invocations in the inspected path. These persistence characteristics already existed before this PR.

**Hardening Proposals**
- _proposed_ — Separate transient request cookies from every durable jar save, including request-level serialization. Validate restart and overlapping same-account invocation behavior so the POST-only guarantee does not depend solely on final cleanup.
