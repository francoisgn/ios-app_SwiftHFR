## Problem
`URLSessionTransportTests.silentServerTimesOut()` failed once on Linux on a docs-only PR (job 134459613258): `transport(code: -1011)` instead of `timedOut`. A red HFRKit job blocks every merge.

## Cause
Reproduced, not assumed. `LoopbackServer`'s silent reply (`hangs`) slept 5 s then closed the connection. In the failing run every test « passed after 58.5 seconds »: the whole process stalled (the same ~55 s stall `dev-swift` shows when the 2,100 tests start together). When the client's executor cannot run for longer than the server's 5 s, the server's close reaches the delegate **before** the idle watcher (a pool task) can run, so the request ends with the connection error (`-1011`) and not the timeout. Reproduced by blocking every thread of the cooperative pool for 6 s after the server had received the request: `transport(code: -1011)`, 3 runs out of 3 (and by the reviewer on `origin/dev-swift`, 3 out of 3; round 2 re-ran it end to end against the fix: 16/16 `timedOut`).

## Change
- **Test server** (`LoopbackServer`): a silent reply (`hangs`, and the stalled answer) keeps the connection open until the **client** closes it (safety timeout 300 s); a server that closes first is what raced. The `dropsAfter` option is removed (no test uses it).
- **Transport**: `IdleWatchDelegate` (Linux only, own file, injectable clock). When the connection ends with an error and the network had sent nothing for the idle limit or more, the error is `URLError(.timedOut)`, measured by the delegate when the connection ends, not by the watcher (which may not be able to run). Two clocks: `lastReceived` (start, response, data) is what the mapping reads; `lastActivity` is the watcher's. A cancellation stays a cancellation; same one-millisecond floor as the watcher. Native `URLSession` (Apple) already reports a timeout at the limit. Risk, stated: after a process-wide stall a genuine error that surfaces late can read as `timedOut` instead of `transport`; both are failures.
- **No executor-starving tests**: the cooperative pool is process-wide, so such a test stalls its neighbours (measured).
- **Second flake, found by the 200-run loop** (existing before this change): `refusedConnectionIsATransportError` connected to a port freed by a stopped server, which a parallel test could be given. `RefusingPort.baseURL` is now `http://127.0.0.1:1`: privileged, never an ephemeral port, refused (reviewer: 0.4 ms); a bound, non-listening socket would be dropped without a reset on macOS.

## Tests
- `IdleWatchDelegateTests` (Linux, 7 tests, clock moved by hand): drop after the limit is a timeout; the watcher's restart does not hide the silence; an error before the limit stays a transport error; data restarts the silence; a cancellation after a long silence stays a cancellation; a zero limit; an answer after a long silence stays an answer. Each fails without its piece (mutations, re-run by the reviewer).
- `silentServerTimesOut` and `answerThatStallsAfterItsFirstBytesFailsAtTheIdleLimit` guard the watcher end to end. Round 3 showed a wall-clock bound cannot: the full suite stalls the process for ~55 s (the `< 30 s` bound failed 4 full runs out of 6). They now use a session whose own `timeoutIntervalForRequest` is an hour, so without the watcher the request cannot end by itself and the 3-minute `.timeLimit` fails the test. **Shown to fail without the watcher** (its `throw URLError(.timedOut)` removed): both fail with « Time limit was exceeded: 180 s ». With the watcher they end in under a second.
- Linux, current head `32f4438`: the two suites 24/24; **full `swift test` 3/3 green (2119 tests, ~55 s each)**; `swiftlint lint --strict` 0 violations. Earlier heads: 200/200 filtered loop under CPU load on `59a161f`; round 3's reviewer ran 25 filtered runs under load green.

**Revue adversariale (Opus 5.5) : CHANGEMENTS DEMANDÉS** — tête b353877e

1. **Moyenne** — `URLSessionTransport.swift`, `didCompleteWithError` (the `silence` measurement) and the watcher's stall heuristic (`if slept >= … { delegate.noteActivity() }`). Both use the same `lastActivity`. When the pool is starved for longer than `idle`, the watcher wakes and resets the clock first. A server drop that arrives less than `idle` after that is then measured as a short silence and gives `transport(code: -1011)`, although the connection was silent for more than 1 s.
   - Scenario: `ServerReply(dropsAfter: 1.1)`, `timeout: 0.2`, `activeProcessorCount` blockers of 1.0 s each.
   - Result: `-1011` in 5/5 runs at each of 1.05, 1.1 and 1.15 s (15/15). At 1.2 s it passes.
   - The PR's claim that "a quiet connection dropped after the limit is a timeout while the watcher cannot run" does not hold just after the stall ends.
   - Fix: add a `lastReceived` clock that only `start` and `didReceive` (response and data) update, and use it in the mapping. I tried this: the scenario passes 10/10 and `HFRNetworkTests` stays green (207 tests). Add that scenario as a test.
2. **Basse** — the `!cancelled &&` guard in the same function is not tested. With it removed, all 207 `HFRNetworkTests` stay green, because `cancellingTheTaskThrowsCancelled` cancels after 0.3 s with an idle limit of 10 s. Fix: a test that cancels from outside the pool while the pool is blocked, after more than `idle` of silence.
3. **Détail** — the mapping uses the raw `idle`, while the watcher uses `max(idle, 0.001)`. With `timeout: 0`, every error, a refused connection included, becomes `timedOut`. Fix: use the same floor in both places.
