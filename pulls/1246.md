### Problem
On a device whose calendar is Islamic, Hebrew or Persian, 10 December 2026 is not in month 12 of that calendar: the seasonal Noël icons (window 01/12–26/12) were not offered while the Noël theme was, they were offered on days 1–26 of that calendar's 12th month, `AppIconSeasonModel.checkAtLaunch` asked to revert in mid-December, and the `<icon>:<year>` « already answered » token used that calendar's year.

### Cause
`IconWindow.contains` and `lastEndYear` read month, day and year with the calendar they are given, and the two models default to `Calendar.current` (their only construction sites pass none). The seasonal theme goes through `NoelCalendar.gregorian(in:)` ("season dates and years never follow a Buddhist or Japanese device calendar"); the icons did not. The icon tests build a Gregorian calendar everywhere, so `.current` was never exercised.

### Change
`IconWindow.contains` and `lastEndYear` convert through `NoelCalendar.gregorian(in: calendar.timeZone)`: the day and the year are Gregorian, the time zone is still the given calendar's. Both models, the picker's offered icons and the season check go through those two functions (the reviewer checked there is no other date read in the icon path), so nothing else changes. No new API, no engine change. `docs/ICONS.md` now says the windows are Gregorian (review round 1).

One consequence, no migration needed: a tester on a Buddhist or Japanese calendar who answered « Revenir à l'icône précédente ? » in a pre-fix build stored a token with that calendar's year (`<icon>:2569`); the corrected token (`<icon>:2026`) asks that tester once more.

### Tests
`AppIconCalendarTests` (written first): for Islamic Umm al-Qura, Hebrew, Persian, Buddhist and Japanese device calendars, the Noël window contains 10 Dec 2026 and not 10 Jun 2026; `lastEndYear` is 2026 on 3 Jan 2027 and on 27 Dec 2026 and 2025 on 10 Dec 2026; and the picker (`AppIconSettingsModel`, the real model) offers the Noël icons on 10 Dec and not on 10 Jun for the Islamic, Hebrew and Persian calendars. Before the change (the reviewer ran Foundation on Linux): all Islamic, Hebrew and Persian rows fail (10 Dec 2026 is 1/7/1448, 30/3/5787, 19/9/1405; 10 Jun 2026 is 24/12/1447 in the Islamic calendar), and every `lastEndYear` row fails, Buddhist and Japanese included (2569 / year 8); only the Buddhist and Japanese window rows share Gregorian months and days and pass before the change, as a guard. The Compile check was green on c8516d8c; the app tests are not run here (Linux).
