## What and why

Three app tests failed intermittently under CI load and turned PRs and `dev-swift` red. No test is skipped, loosened or retried: two causes are in the product, one in the test.

### 1. `TopicRowActionsTests.aRemovalInFlightIsNotSentTwice` (#564) — race in the test
- **Evidence**: jobs 132205936656 (#873), 132349474796, 132363675733 (#887), 132204636320 (`dev-swift`): every failure is `writer.removed.count → 0` at line 226, never 2. The second tap did not send; the first had not arrived yet.
- **Cause**: `removeFlag` inserts the row into `removingFlags` on the main actor, then awaits `TopicFlagActions.removeFavorite`, a nonisolated `async` function (the target sets no default isolation and no `NonisolatedNonsendingByDefault`). So the request reaches `FakeFlagWriter` from the global executor, later. The test waited on `isRemovingFlag`, which is set before the hop, so on a loaded runner the second tap and the count ran in between.
- **Change**: the test waits for the request at the writer (`writer.removed` not empty), checks the row is still marked "removing", then taps again. It has a `.timeLimit(.minutes(1))`. Assertions unchanged; the product code was right.

### 2. `ReaderInsetsWebTests.aRefreshReopensAtTheSameMessageWhenTheBarsChanged` (under #772) — product race with WebKit's reset
- **Evidence**: jobs 132226381251, 132349474796, 132204636320 all fail at line 162 with `scrollY after load: 0.0`, after line 157 had seen the restored `scrollY == 1000`. So the restore happened and was **undone to exactly the top**. That is WebKit's reset of a new document; a late layout or the old offset would read 1000.
- **Cause**: WebKit resets a new document's scroll view to its top when it applies the first layer-tree commit after the navigation's commit. That usually happens **after** the page reports ready: the test's #642 comment notes that at ready the scroll view still shows the old document's 900 offset. `reader.js` delays the scroll by two animation frames (#642), but the reset reaches the page from the UI process's scroll update, and on a loaded runner that update arrived after the two frames.
- **Change**: `WebKitResetWatch` (new, `App/SwiftHFR/Reader/WebKitResetWatch.swift`) holds a new document's landing or restore until the reset.
  - **Start**: the watch starts at the page navigation's commit (`webView(_:didCommit:)`). It watches `contentOffset` by KVO and sees the reset as the scroll view arriving at its top.
  - **At ready**:
    - reset already seen: the scroll runs at once;
    - scroll view still away from its top (the previous document's offset): the reset is certain to move it, so the scroll waits for it;
    - scroll view already at its top: the reset may have come without moving it, so the scroll runs at once **and once more** if the reset comes later.
  - **End**: the watch is not armed while the reader scrolls at the commit. It is ended by a drag or fling, by the reset, and by the next `show` (the task queued at the reset checks the token). The at-top second chance also ends at the page's first position report, so a later status-bar or VoiceOver scroll to the top cannot replay the landing.
  - **Scope**: the landing on an anchor takes the same path (same exposure). Scroll requests inside a shown document (`apply(_:)`) are unchanged.

### 3. `ReaderInsetsWebTests.aRestoreWhileHiddenDoesNotKeepTheControllerAlive` (#772) — product: a pending page call held the controller
- **Evidence**: the test has not failed in the five jobs above, which all ran after #841 rewrote it. Its earlier failures in #772 (runs 42902785467, 42840450745, 42890094480) were the deallocation check.
- **Cause, from the code**: every fire-and-forget page call (`scroll(to:)`, `scroll(toReadingTop:)`, `applyTextScale`, `applyTheme`, `requestSelectionQuote`) was `Task { try? await webView.callAsyncJavaScript(…) }`. That task holds `self` **until the page answers**, and a page out of its window may not answer (suspended process). The controller is then never freed, which is what this test checks.
- **Change**: one `run(_:arguments:)` helper calls the completion-handler API with no handler. It captures nothing and waits for nothing, and all five call sites use it.

## How it was verified
- **CI on `f13616e`** (app job 132977268371): the app built, and the three tests passed:
  - `aRemovalInFlightIsNotSentTwice`
  - `aRefreshReopensAtTheSameMessageWhenTheBarsChanged`
  - `aRestoreWhileHiddenDoesNotKeepTheControllerAlive`

  The only failure was `MessageTitleToneTests` (#704, unrelated).
- **New `WebKitResetWatchTests`** run on a plain `UIScrollView`, with no page and no WebKit. They check:
  - a held action runs once at the reset, never before, never twice;
  - a drag drops it;
  - a commit while scrolling arms nothing;
  - `stop()` (another document) drops it, also once queued;
  - the at-top second chance, and its end at a position report;
  - `WebKitResetWatchControllerTests` checks that `show` stops the watch.
- **Each guard has a test that fails without it**: the drag check, the commit-time drag guard, the token check in the queued task, and `stop()` in `show`.
- **Not verifiable from Linux**: whether WebKit's reset of a scroll view already at its top fires a KVO notification (an equal value). If it does not, the at-top case keeps the earlier behaviour (scroll at once), not worse.
