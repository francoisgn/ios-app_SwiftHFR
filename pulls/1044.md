## Problem
`ReaderInsetsWebTests.aRefreshReopensAtTheSameMessageWhenTheBarsChanged()` (and its pinch variant) failed now and then on CI after #996. In job 133859276257 the restore was seen at `scrollY` 1000 on both sides, then the page was back at 0 once loaded. Job 133772120017 had the same shape. Under the test, the product path is the one a refresh or a landing takes: the reader can reopen at the top instead of where the member was.

## Cause (measured on the Mac, 100 runs, traces on #995)
WebKit's reset of a new document reaches the scroll view (UI process) and the page (WebContent) at different times. In 3 of the traced runs, the "reset" is the scroll view going to its top and, **in the same UI turn (< 0.1 ms)**, back to the page's own inherited offset. So the scroll view reaching its top does not mean the page was reset, and the two sides reconcile through asynchronous messages afterwards. `WebKitResetWatch` sees only the scroll view. A restore sent once the scroll view was reset could therefore be undone when the reset reached the page later. Not reproduced locally in 100 runs (CPU load included); the tests below deliver that late reset on purpose.

## Change
- `reader.js`: `afterAgreement(from, scroll)`. After the two frames of #642, a scroll the app asks for also waits until the page's `scrollY` shows `from`, the scroll view's reading top when the app asked, so the reset has reached the page. It waits at most 30 frames, then scrolls anyway, so it delays a scroll but never drops one. `restoreScroll(y, from)` and `landOnPost(n, from)` use it; without `from` (a direct jump, `scrollToPost`) nothing changes.
- `ReaderWebController+Scroll`: the restore and the landing pass `from` (`readingTop`, CSS pixels). A jump inside the shown document is unchanged.
- **Diagnostic (operator: both in one PR):** `OffsetHistory` records every offset the scroll view takes, timed. The two refresh tests put it in their failure messages, so a CI failure shows the order of the reset, the page's scrolls and the restore.

Why no wider: the watch's own logic is unchanged. The two-frame wait is kept, so the new wait only starts after it. Only the two app-asked scrolls that follow a new document get it.

## Tests
`ReaderResetAgreementWebTests` (new, real `WKWebView`):
- `aRestoreWaitsForTheResetThenRunsAtOnce`: page at 300, restore asked with the scroll view at the top (`from` 0), reset delivered 5 frames later. Checks that the page **waited** (300 at frame 5) and that the restore **ran within 3 frames** of the page agreeing.
- `aLandingWaitsForTheResetThenRunsAtOnce`: the same for a landing on message 2.
- `aRestoreWhoseResetNeverReachesThePageStillScrolls`: the 30-frame cap. It is a guard against the wait dropping a scroll.
- `theMemberTouchingThePageDuringTheWaitCancelsTheRestore` and `aTouchInTheSameTurnAsTheAskCancelsTheRestore`: touches are counted, not timed (WebKit coarsens `performance.now()`).
- `noAgreementIsAskedWhileTheMemberScrolls`: while `resetWatch.userScrolls` (the one drag predicate), the page gets `null`.
- `theRestoreAndTheLandingGetTheScrollViewsReadingTop(zoom: 1, 2)`: the native `from`. The expectation is taken in the same main-actor turn as the calls, because WebKit may reset the zoom during an await.
